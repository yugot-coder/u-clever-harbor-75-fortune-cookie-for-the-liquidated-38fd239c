Competition: `u-clever-harbor-75-fortune-cookie-for-the-liquidated-38fd239c`
Gate: 1–160 characters, one line, at most 20 words, plain text.
Judge: a freshly liquidated crypto trader, 3 a.m., empty wallet, stubborn
sense of humour. It must sound like a real fortune AND land the joke on
the loss — rueful and warm, never cruel or smug — with a fresh turn, a
double meaning, or a surprising last word.

## Picked: #1

"Fortune favors the bold. Tonight it favored the exchange, and the exchange kept the change."

Why it wins against the field:

- Cookie voice + loss, both present (criterion 1): it opens on the most
  classic fortune-cookie proverb there is, and the loss is the whole
  second sentence — fortune went to the exchange, and the exchange kept
  the trader's money.
- Warm, rueful laugh, not a lecture (criterion 2): the trader is not
  blamed, pitied, or called stupid. Fortune itself defected to the other
  side; the trader and the cookie share the joke.
- One breath (criterion 3): 15 words, 91 characters, two short sentences.
- Fresh turn (criterion 4): "kept the change" is a double meaning —
  the exchange kept the trader's money as its change, and kept the
  (spare) change like a tip jar. The last word lands the joke.
- Fortune voice, then the punchline breaks it (criterion 5): a solemn
  proverb, subverted by the second sentence.

Why not the others: #2 is clever but "yours was open" carries a
told-you-so edge; #4 leans on the obvious margin-call word; #5 and #6
name the loss directly but their twists are thinner; #3, #7, #8, #9, #10
are all warm but their last words do not hit as hard as "the change".
